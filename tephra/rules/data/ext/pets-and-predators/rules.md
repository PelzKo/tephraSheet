## Pets & Predators

### Animal basics
- **Five aspects** of an animal: (1) size tier + niche, (2) movement trait, (3) special traits, (4) secondary traits (= derived-stat allocations), (5) stories.
- **Taming**: Taming Tier = SP (+1 per SP). Step 6 says "taming tier = its special traits unless otherwise stated". The animal must be held captive. Once per downtime one tamer rolls Expertise or Shamanism and lowers the taming tier by the tier of the result. Re-roll abilities: the tamer keeps the higher result (T1, then a T2 re-roll → −2). At 0 the animal is tamed. Taking over an animal that someone else tamed (and that refuses) = tame it as if wild, and it loses training benefits from the other tamer (narrator may waive). **Social** trait: tamed as if it had ceil(SP/2).
- **Buying**: price per SP by SP marque: Mq1 15 pr, Mq2 75 pr, Mq3 375 pr, Mq4 1875 pr (examples: Mq1 with 1 SP = 15 pr; Mq2 with 5 SP = 375 pr). Training stories add their listed cost. **Selling** = 1/5 of that.
- **Leveling up**: apply derived stats as when adding a special trait. Some specialties let animals level with the character.
- **Captivity**: held so it cannot escape during a breather/downtime. Whoever holds it gets the captivity benefits (taming, Sedative, Captive Animal Adjustments).
- **Venom prices** (permit officially needed; frontier: false permits are cheap): Mq1 12 pr, Mq2 60 pr, Mq3 300 pr, Mq4 1500 pr. Venom without a price = Mq2.
- **Ordinary actions**: any Playing Guide action except item use or social tells. Also Brute/Spirit attribute actions, Notice, Balance, Jumping, Sneaking. Attacks: unarmed and trait attacks. **All animals have 3 AP** (training stories raise this). Animal grab attacks cost 2 AP (bite/claw) unless stated otherwise.
- **Tamed animals / directives**: the owner spends **1 AP** for a simple directive (attack X, come, hide, stalk…). The animal acts right before or any time after the owner's turn (the owner picks when priority is rolled). Sequences need one directive each, unless the animal has Sequenced Orders. Tamed animals may give AP as an ally (e.g. Faith's Smite). All of one player's tamed animals count as **one ally**. Party-tamed animals have one primary controller, and others control them only with that player's consent.
- **Animal essence**: animals have **3 regular essence slots** (Manipulate Essence works on them). "Animal Essence Augment" = animal-only. Prosthetic limbs work as normal.

### Acquiring an animal at character creation (p.5)
An Animal Tamer can start with a premade animal with **SP = floor(specialties known / 2)** (at level 1 this is 1). A **small** animal gets +1 SP. These SP must come only from special traits. If the tamer is also a Bio-Engineer, the bio-engineered animal replaces this one. SP from training/other traits are added afterwards. Narrator note: the narrator may let the player create the animal themselves (checks that it fits the lore).

### Stampedes (p.9, new rules)
| Tier | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | T1 Brute or knocked prone; stampede +0 Stk |
| 2 | T1 Brute or knocked prone; +10 Stk |
| 3 | T1 Brute or knocked prone; +20 Stk |
| 4 | T1 Brute or knocked prone; +30 Stk |

When hit by a stampede, and every turn inside it: Brute roll or prone. A prone person must roll that Brute to stand. Each turn being trampled, the stampede attacks: damage class by animal size (rats 2, dogs 4, tigers 6, cows 8, elephants 10), Stk by tier. **Calming**: 3 AP, any fitting attribute. Each tier above the stampede's tier lowers it by 1 (vs a T3 stampede: T4 roll → T2; then T3 roll → T1).

### Self-Made Immunity & Immunizations with venoms (p.22)
1. An alchemist who has a venom and can immunize may spend one use of that venom to grant resistance to that venom type.
2. Resistant to a venom type: +1 tier to resist that type, whoever uses it.
3. Venom resistance applies once per venom type.
4. It applies to all instances of that venom type, including mixes (Venom Chemist) and potions, but not to the other effects of those mixes/potions.
5. You cannot be immunized against "Venomized" as such, only against the individual venoms it introduces.
